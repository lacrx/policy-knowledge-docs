## Detailed Country Profiles

### Very Light / Free Market

**UK (England/Wales)** — No rent control since Housing Act 1988. Renters' Rights Act 2025 (effective May 2026): one rent increase per year, 2 months notice, tribunal challenge. No caps. Scotland: temporary 3% cap (Sept 2022-March 2024, now expired); new Housing Scotland Bill proposes permanent system.

**Finland** — Free market. Initial rents freely negotiated. Tribunal oversight for disputes. Housing First addresses homelessness through institutional supply, not price regulation.

**Czech Republic** — Fully deregulated since 2014. Prague vacancy 2-4%; rents up ~9.5% YoY Q1 2025. Prague/Brno rents absorb ~60% of median income. No reregulation planned.

**Hungary** — Contractual only. No mandated increase mechanism. +231% house price increase 2014-2024. Post-privatization free market with no supply pipeline.

### Light to Moderate

**Norway** — Rent "may not unreasonably exceed similar rentals." Annual CPI adjustment if either party requests. ~79% homeownership; thin rental market. OECD flagged need for zoning reform.

**Belgium** — Health index (CPI minus energy/tobacco) during tenancy. Initial rent free. Rent revisable if property value changes 20%+ or energy efficiency improves 10%. Brussels has stricter regional rules.

**Italy** — Dual track: (1) *Canone libero* (4+4 years) — free rent, increases at 75% of ISTAT inflation. (2) *Canone concordato* (3+2 years) — locally agreed regulated rent with 10% cedolare secca flat tax incentive. Constitutional Court requires landlords must cover maintenance costs.

**Poland** — Increases max once per 6 months. Above 3% of replacement value requires justification. Private market largely free. Building 287K permits/year — highest in EU.

**Switzerland** — Rents tied to SARON-based mortgage reference rate. Landlords can raise 3% per 0.25% rate increase. Inflation pass-through cut from 40% to 28% in April 2024. Zurich vacancy 0.07%. New tenants pay **70% premium** over sitting tenants for same apartment. 2014 Spatial Planning Act capped new zoning — structural constraint.

### Moderate to High

**Portugal** — Annual CPI-linked via INE coefficient. 2023: emergency 2% cap. 2024: cap dropped entirely, 6.94% increase allowed. 2025: 2.16% (INE coefficient). Replacement: demand-side subsidies (up to €200/month if rent >35% of income). House prices +124% from 2015-2024.

**Ireland** — RPZs: annual increase capped at lower of CPI or 2%. New-build exemption (post-June 2025). RPZs replaced Feb 2026 with permanent national system; 6-year reset for landlords. Rents up 115% 2010-2025. Rental income taxed up to 52% marginal → landlord exodus. Paradox: highest EU construction rate (6.2/1000) AND 25.3/10K homelessness.

**Austria** — Three MRG categories: (1) Full MRG (pre-1945/1953): benchmark rent per state (Vienna €6.74/m²). (2) Partial MRG (post-1953): CPI with cap — 3% passes through fully; above 3%, only half. (3) Exempt: single-family, owner-occupied. **2026-2027 reform**: hard 1%/2% caps (5th Rent Law Inflation Mitigation Act, Dec 2025). Vienna's affordability comes from 420K social housing units and payroll levy, not from rent law.

**Denmark** — Cost-based regulation on pre-1991 stock (~75% of private rentals). Social housing 21% of stock — no income threshold (universalist). Copenhagen waits 20+ years for desirable locations. Emergency 4% cap in 2023. OECD (Jan 2026) recommends aligning with market, replacing with targeted allowances. "Blackstone intervention" (2019): 5-year moratorium on post-renovation rent hikes.

**Germany** — Mietpreisbremse (2015): new leases in tight markets capped at 10% above local Mietspiegel. In-tenancy: max 20% in 3 years (15% in tight markets). Anti-usury: >20% above comparable = criminal. Berlin Mietendeckel (2020 state-level freeze) struck down by Federal Constitutional Court April 2021 — states lack housing competence. **Rent II bill (2026)**: index-linked contracts in tight markets capped — CPI above 3%/year, only half passes through. Furnishing surcharges disclosed/limited. Covers 13 of 16 federal states. Germany builds 260K vs 400K target — each regulatory tightening followed by further supply shortfall.

**France** — IRL index-linked nationwide. *Encadrement des loyers* in tense zones (Paris, Lyon, Lille, Montpellier, Bordeaux, Basque Country, Grenoble-Alpes): new rents within reference rent ±20%/−30%. F/G energy-rated homes: zero increase since August 2022. Fines: €5,000 individual, €15,000 corporate. Extended through November 2026 (3DS law). Wealthy communes pay SRU fines rather than build required 25% social housing.

**Spain** — Housing Law 12/2023: CPI cap during 5-year tenancy (3% in 2024). Stressed areas: new rent cannot exceed 5-year high (individual landlords); large holders (10+ properties) must comply with national index. New IRAV/RIUR index from Jan 2025. Catalonia implementing aggressively; Madrid/Andalucía/Valencia ignoring. Constitutionality challenged by PP.

### High / Most Restrictive

**Netherlands** — Points-based WWS: social (≤143 pts) max €933/month; mid-segment (144-186 pts) max €1,228/month; free (187+ pts) no cap. **Affordable Rent Act (July 2024)**: extended regulation to mid-market — biggest expansion since 1979. ~90% of rental stock now regulated. Immediate result: **38% drop in free-sector listings** (NVM Q4 2025); 42% of private rentals above €2,000/month. Government announced loosening April 2026 — less than 2 years after enactment. OECD warned of supply contraction (Nov 2025).

**Sweden** — Bruksvärdessystemet: ALL rentals regulated via collective bargaining. No free-market segment. No vacancy decontrol. OECD highest restrictiveness score. 9-year Stockholm queue, 894K nationally registered, €110M/yr black market, homicides over contracts. See sweden-housing-model.

## The Vacancy Decontrol Divide

The single most important design feature separating functional from dysfunctional rent regulation:

**With vacancy decontrol** (rent resets to market between tenancies): Oregon, California, Germany (partial), most European index-linked systems. Allows prices to signal scarcity. Supply responds. Sitting tenants protected; new tenants face market rates.

**Without vacancy decontrol** (rent carries over between tenancies): NYC (post-HSTPA 2019), Sweden, Netherlands (regulated segment). Produces rationing, queues, black markets, misallocation. Landlords cannot recover costs → conversion/exit.

**Key insight from the data**: Whether a country has rent regulation matters less than whether it has vacancy decontrol. Germany (Mietpreisbremse with new-build exemption) functions differently from Sweden (bruksvärdessystemet with no exemption) despite both being "rent controlled."

## US Comparison

| Jurisdiction | System | Key Detail |
|-------------|--------|-----------|
| Oregon (statewide, 2019) | 7% + CPI | 15-year new construction exemption. Vacancy decontrol. |
| California (AB 1482, statewide) | 5% + local CPI (max 10%) | Multi-family pre-2010. Expires 2030. Vacancy decontrol. Local cities (LA, SF) stricter. |
| New York City | Hard ceiling | 45% of rentals stabilized. **No vacancy decontrol** (HSTPA 2019). Functionally closest to Sweden. |
| ~30 states | Preemption | State laws ban local rent control. |

US social housing: ~1-2% of stock (vs EU avg 8%). No institutional backstop. US rent control is local-only and lacks the large social housing sector that makes European systems partially functional.

## ECHR Constitutional Floor

*Hutten-Czapska v. Poland* (2006): ECHR ruled rent control cannot be so extreme that maintenance costs exceed rental income with no legal remedy. Binding on all Council of Europe member states. Sets a floor below which regulation becomes a property rights violation.

Germany, France, and Italy constitutional courts all permit rent control but require: (1) not rendering property "economically useless," (2) temporality or proportionality, (3) allowing landlords to earn a return.

## Key Analytical Findings

1. **Free markets don't guarantee affordability**: UK and Czech Republic have minimal regulation and severe housing crises. The variable that predicts outcomes is supply permissiveness, not regulation presence/absence.

2. **Vacancy decontrol is the critical design feature**: Systems with it (Germany, Oregon, California) function differently from systems without it (Sweden, NYC, Netherlands regulated sector).

3. **Rent regulation without institutional supply fails**: Sweden controls prices without building alternatives. Vienna controls prices AND builds 420K social units. Same label, opposite mechanisms.

4. **Every tightening triggers supply contraction**: Netherlands 2024, Germany 2026, Ireland RPZs, Spain stressed zones — pattern is consistent. The question is whether the contraction is worth the tenant protection.

5. **Quebec TAL is the best model**: Guideline-based, no vacancy decontrol but soft enforcement, supply elasticity maintained. See montreal-housing-model.
