# California Public Records Act — Compliance & Strategy

## Overview

The California Public Records Act (Gov. Code §§ 7920.000-7931.000, reorganized from §§ 6250-6270 effective Jan 1, 2023) establishes the public's right to access government records. Every person has a right to inspect any public record — the burden is on the agency to justify withholding, not on the requester to justify asking.

---

## Key Statutory Provisions

| Provision | Code Section | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Right of access** | § 7921.000 | Public records are open to inspection at all times during office hours |
| **Response deadline** | § 7922.535(a) | Agency must determine within **10 calendar days** whether request seeks disclosable records |
| **Extension** | § 7922.535(b) | May extend deadline by up to **14 additional days** with written notice stating reasons |
| **Format** | § 7922.570 | Must provide in requested format if records exist in that format |
| **Fees** | § 7922.530 | Limited to direct duplication costs; no search fees, no compilation fees |
| **Email/electronic** | § 7922.570(b) | Electronic records must be provided electronically if requested |
| **Partial disclosure** | § 7922.525 | Must redact exempt portions and release the rest — cannot withhold entire document because part is exempt |

---

## Response Timeline

```
Day 0:  Request submitted
Day 10: Agency must respond (determination + estimated date for production)
        ├── "Records available" → produce promptly
        ├── "Need extension" → 14 more calendar days (must state reason)
        └── "Request denied" → must cite specific exemption
Day 24: Extended deadline expires — records must be produced or denial finalized
```

**"Unusual circumstances" for extension (§ 7922.535(b)):**
- Need to search/collect from field facilities
- Voluminous request requiring separation of exempt from non-exempt
- Need to consult with another agency
- Need to compile data or create a report (limited — they don't have to create NEW records)

## What's Exempt (Common Categories)

| Exemption | Code Section | What It Covers |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Deliberative process** | § 7927.500 | Drafts, notes, preliminary recommendations (NOT final decisions) |
| **Attorney-client** | § 7927.705 | Communications with city attorney for legal advice |
| **Personnel records** | § 7927.700 | Employee evaluations, discipline (but NOT salary, title, or duties) |
| **Pending litigation** | § 7927.705 | Records pertaining to pending or anticipated litigation |
| **Law enforcement** | §§ 7923.600-7923.625 | Active investigation records |
| **Real estate negotiations** | § 7927.100 | Price/terms of pending property acquisitions |

**Critical limits on exemptions:**
- Agency bears the burden of proof that exemption applies
- Must demonstrate specific harm from disclosure, not just assert the category
- Partial redaction required — cannot withhold entire document for one exempt paragraph
- Exemptions are narrowly construed; public access is the default

---

## What's NOT Exempt (Common Targets for Advocacy)

- **Council member emails** about public business on personal accounts — public records (City of San Jose v. Superior Court, 2017)
- **Staff reports and recommendations** — final products, not deliberative
- **Contracts and MOUs** — always public
- **Meeting recordings and minutes** — always public
- **Permit applications and plan sets** — public records once submitted
- **Budget documents** — always public
- **Correspondence with developers, lobbyists, constituents** about pending decisions — generally public
- **GIS data and mapping** — public (Gov. Code § 7922.565)

## Writing Effective Requests

### Principles

1. **Be specific enough to be actionable** — vague requests ("all records about housing") invite delay. Name the project, date range, department, or document type.
2. **Be broad enough to catch evasion** — agencies interpret requests literally. "Emails from the planning director" misses emails FROM developers TO the director. Request "all correspondence between or among" specified parties.
3. **Cite the statute** — establishes you know the law. Agencies are less likely to stall experienced requesters.
4. **Request electronic format** — prevents the "1,000 pages at $0.25/page" gambit. Electronic copies have no per-page cost.
5. **Set a date range** — prevents "this is too voluminous" extensions. 90 days is usually sufficient; expand if needed.
6. **Name the records, not the conclusions** — don't ask "records showing the city violated HAA." Ask "all correspondence, staff reports, and council communications regarding [Project X] between [dates]."

### Structure

```
[Date]
[Agency name and address]

RE: California Public Records Act Request

Dear Records Custodian:

Pursuant to the California Public Records Act (Gov. Code § 7921.000 et seq.),
I request the following records:

1. [Specific record type] regarding [subject] for the period [start] to [end].
2. [Specific record type]...

Please provide records in electronic format per Gov. Code § 7922.570(b).

Per Gov. Code § 7922.535(a), I expect a determination within 10 calendar days
of receipt of this request.

[Name]
[Contact information]
```

## When They Stall

**No response within 10 days:**
- Send follow-up citing § 7922.535(a) and noting the deadline has passed
- CC the city clerk and city attorney

**"We need an extension" without statutory basis:**
- Respond requesting the specific "unusual circumstance" under § 7922.535(b)
- An extension must be in writing with reasons

**Excessive fee demands:**
- CPRA limits fees to direct duplication costs (§ 7922.530)
- No search fees. No staff time fees. No "compilation" fees.
- Electronic copies: effectively zero marginal cost
- Respond citing the statute and requesting electronic format

**"Records don't exist":**
- Ask for the agency's records retention schedule
- Ask whether records were destroyed, and if so, the destruction date and authority
- File for related records that would reference the target (meeting minutes, agenda packets, email chains)

**Blanket exemption claim without specificity:**
- Agency must identify the specific exemption and demonstrate harm
- Respond requesting: which exemption, applied to which records, what specific harm from disclosure
- Remind of partial disclosure obligation (§ 7922.525)

## Enforcement

- **Administrative complaint**: file with the agency head
- **Superior Court petition**: Gov. Code § 7923.000 — court orders disclosure; agency pays petitioner's attorney's fees if petitioner prevails
- **Attorney's fees**: mandatory if requester substantially prevails (§ 7923.115)
- **First Amendment Coalition**: offers guidance and sometimes representation for significant cases
