# Singapore HDB Model — Government as Master Developer

## Overview

Singapore has the most successful public housing program in the world by coverage: 77.2% of the resident population lives in Housing Development Board (HDB) flats, with a 91.2% homeownership rate — including 84% among the poorest decile. The system achieves racial integration, affordability (price-to-income ratio of 4.4x for a 4-room flat), and near-universal housing access.

It accomplishes this through three mechanisms no other country has combined: government ownership of ~90% of land (via the 1966 Land Acquisition Act), mandatory savings at 37% of wages (Central Provident Fund), and a single government entity that plans, builds, sells, and manages housing for 5.6 million people across 23 new towns.

The model is widely studied and almost entirely non-replicable. It requires city-state governance with no federal/state/local tension, single-party dominance enabling 50-year planning horizons, compulsory savings at rates no Western democracy could impose, and eminent domain powers that would be unconstitutional elsewhere. What transfers is not the system but specific design principles: mixed-income integration, government as developer, leasehold for land value capture, and housing-as-infrastructure framing.

## How HDB Works

### Structure

All HDB flats are sold on **99-year leasehold**. The government retains land ownership and common areas. At lease expiry, the flat reverts to the state at zero value. HDB manages ~1.148 million dwelling units across 23 new towns, each housing up to 300,000 residents.

### BTO (Build-to-Order) vs. Resale

**BTO flats**: Government-built, sold at subsidized prices with fresh 99-year lease. Since October 2024, classified into three tiers:

| Tier | Location | MOP | Subsidy Recovery on Resale |
|------|----------|-----|---------------------------|
| Standard | Non-central | 5 years | None |
| Plus | Near MRT/town centers | 10 years | 6-8% |
| Prime | Central/highly desirable | 10 years | 9% |

Plus and Prime flats priced ~35-40% below comparable resale HDB. Wait times: 3-5 years from application to key collection; 80% under 4 years as of 2025.

**Resale flats**: Market-driven prices, immediate occupancy, no income ceiling. Median resale price: S$628,000 (2025).

### Income Ceilings (2025-2026)

| Category | Ceiling |
|----------|---------|
| BTO (3-5 room, families) | S$14,000/month |
| BTO (2-room Flexi, singles) | S$7,000/month |
| Multi-generation | S$21,000/month |
| Executive Condominium | S$16,000/month |
| Resale HDB | No ceiling |

### Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP)

Introduced 1989 to prevent racial enclaves. Quotas enforced at both block and neighborhood level:

| Ethnic Group | Block Limit | Neighborhood Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese | 84% | 78% |
| Malay | 22% | 16% |
| Indian/Others | 12% | 10% |

Both limits must be satisfied for any transaction. If either is at capacity for a buyer's ethnic group, the sale is blocked regardless of price. Every HDB block is a microcosm of Singapore's ethnic composition.

## CPF Funding Mechanism

### How It Works

The Central Provident Fund is a mandatory savings scheme with total contributions of **37% of wages** for workers under 55 (20% employee + 17% employer). The Ordinary Account (OA) receives 23% of wages for workers under 35, front-loaded to support first home purchases.

CPF-OA funds can pay for down payment, stamp duty, and monthly mortgage installments. For HDB flats, up to 100% of purchase price can come from OA savings. Mortgage Servicing Ratio capped at 30% of gross income.

**Scale**: US$513 billion under management for 4.2 million account holders (May 2026).

### Why This Matters

CPF functions as a **mandatory pre-funded housing savings program**. Workers accumulate housing funds through payroll, not through direct taxation or general revenue. The employer's 17% contribution is effectively a housing/retirement subsidy built into labor costs. No Western democracy has imposed savings at this rate.

### Comparison to Other Funding Models

| Mechanism | Singapore CPF | Vienna Payroll Levy | Split-Rate LVT |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Rate | 37% total (23% to housing) | 1-1.5% | Variable |
| Purpose | Individual savings for purchase | Collective fund for production | Municipal revenue + incentives |
| Who benefits | Individual buyer | Institutional developers | Development incentive + public revenue |
| Housing model | Ownership | Rental/cooperative | Tenure-neutral |
| Political feasibility elsewhere | Near-zero | Low-moderate | Moderate |

## Outcomes

### Affordability
- Price-to-income ratio: **4.4x** for median 4-room resale (2025)
- Median household income: S$12,446/month
- New BTO 4-room after grants: from S$248,000
- Maximum Enhanced Housing Grant (families): S$120,000

### Homeownership
- **91.2%** of resident households own their home
- Bottom decile: **84%** ownership
- Only 8.2% rent

### Racial Integration
- EIP has prevented racial enclave formation — a persistent problem in other countries' public housing
- Every block is ethnically mixed by law
- Critics note it constrains minority sellers who face smaller buyer pools when quotas are at capacity

### Housing Quality
- SERS (Selective En-bloc Redevelopment Scheme): 82 sites announced since 1995, 77 completed. Residents of aging estates get rebuilt flats with fresh 99-year lease + compensation
- VERS (Voluntary Early Redevelopment Scheme): future program for flats aged 70+
- Ongoing maintenance and upgrading programs across estates

### Homelessness
- 530 rough sleepers recorded (November 2022) in a city of 5.6 million — one of the lowest rates globally

## The Gaps

### 99-Year Lease Depreciation

The system's central tension. At lease expiry, the flat reverts to the government at **zero value**.

- Depreciation curve: 88.5% value at 75 years remaining → 74.7% at 50 years → 54.6% at 25 years
- Banks restrict lending when remaining lease drops below 60 years
- CPF use restricted for flats with <60 years remaining (2013 policy)
- First 33-39 years of ownership capture ~80% of total leasehold value; after that, accelerating decline

Generations were implicitly promised HDB flats would appreciate ("asset enhancement"). This expectation now collides with mathematical reality. Government has begun messaging that flats are "not an appreciating asset" — a politically difficult correction.

### Singles Policy

Unmarried Singaporeans cannot buy BTO flats until age **35**, and only 2-room Flexi units. Resale access also requires 35+. Designed as pro-natalist nudge, but penalizes a growing demographic — the proportion of singles under 50 has increased significantly.

### Foreign Worker Exclusion

Singapore's ~1.4 million foreign workers are entirely excluded from HDB. They live in employer-provided dormitories — conditions exposed during COVID-19: cramped rooms (up to 20-30 men), shared toilets (1 per 80+ workers). Migrant workers accounted for ~90% of Singapore's 60,000 early COVID cases. Post-COVID reforms improved standards but the fundamental exclusion remains.

**The housing miracle for citizens is built on the labor of a permanent underclass with no path to residency or homeownership.**

### Political Control

HDB upgrading programs have been linked to election cycles. The PAP has historically prioritized upgrades in constituencies that voted for them. Lee Kuan Yew openly stated homeownership was intended to give citizens a "stake" and impose discipline. Because government controls supply, pricing, eligibility, and upgrading, housing functions as a lever of political compliance.

### Limited Rental Market

HDB subletting requires 5-year Minimum Occupation Period completion, owner occupancy, and HDB approval. Illegal subletting: fines up to S$200,000. This creates a structurally thin rental market, constraining mobility.

## Five-Pillar Scoring

Using the complete-housing-position framework:

| Pillar | Singapore | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| P1: Legalize | **A** | Government controls all zoning. No NIMBY pathway. But supply is centrally planned, not market-driven. |
| P2: Institutional Capacity | **A+** | HDB is the most capable public housing institution in the world. Plans, builds, sells, manages at national scale. |
| P3: Dedicated Revenue | **A+** | CPF at 37% of wages. Dedicated, mandatory, enormous. But individual savings, not collective fund. |
| P4: Tenant Protections | **C** | Homeownership model sidesteps most tenant issues. Singles penalized. Foreign workers excluded entirely. |
| P5: Construction Costs | **B+** | Vertical integration reduces costs. Competitive bidding with quality metrics. But land cost effectively zero (government owns it). |

**Overall**: Extraordinary institutional capacity producing extraordinary outcomes — for citizens. The system's exclusions (singles, foreign workers) and political control features are not bugs but design choices.

## Transferability Assessment

### Non-Replicable (requires Singapore's unique conditions)

- **Land Acquisition Act**: Government compulsory acquisition at below-market rates. Land ownership went from 49% (1965) to ~90% (2000s). Decisions could not be challenged in court.
- **City-state governance**: One government controls all land use, housing, transport, employment. No jurisdictional fragmentation.
- **Single-party dominance**: PAP governed since independence. Enables 50-year horizons and politically difficult decisions.
- **Mandatory savings at 37%**: Politically impossible in Western democracies. US equivalent would roughly double Social Security contributions.
- **Scale**: 733 km2, 5.6 million people. One housing market with no suburban/rural dynamics.

### Transferable Design Principles

- **Mixed-income, mixed-race integration by design**: EIP concept — mandating diversity at building level. Stronger than US inclusionary zoning.
- **Government as master developer**: Vertical integration from planning to management. Eliminates developer profit margins and coordination failures.
- **Leasehold for land value capture**: 99-year leaseholds allow state to recapture land value. Community land trusts use a version of this.
- **Neighborhood design**: Void decks (communal ground-floor spaces), integrated amenities, "garden city" green space.
- **Housing-as-infrastructure framing**: Treating housing as essential public infrastructure, not a market commodity.

The World Bank's assessment: "sui generis — un-replicable in its entirety" but offers "powerful lessons" in what visionary government investment can achieve.
