# CCC-Oceanside Correspondence: Inclusionary Housing LCPA

Source: California Coastal Commission PRA release (October 2026), CPRA #9. Cross-verified against `policy-knowledge-docs` KB (ca-housing-enforcement, supply-skepticism-rebuttal).

## LCP Amendment: LCP-6-OCN-25-0030-2

Companion to the Downtown Density LCPA. Proposes changes to Chapter 14C (Inclusionary Housing) of the Municipal Code / certified Implementation Plan.

## Key Finding: Uncertified Ordinance Used as Baseline

CCC analyst Belen-Gonzalez discovered that the City's inclusionary housing submittal used ordinance language that was NEVER certified by the Commission. Specifically:

- City Council adopted Ordinance No. 22-OR0848-1 on December 21, 2022
- City Council adopted LCPA22-00003 on January 11, 2023
- Neither was submitted to CCC for certification
- When preparing the current LCPA, staff used a version with all uncertified changes "accepted" as the starting point
- Result: CCC was reviewing proposed changes against a baseline that didn't match the certified LCP

Darlene Nicandro (Jan 8, 2026): "I'm not sure why we only took the City Code Amendment to the City Council on December 21, 2022, rather than package the City Code Amendment with the LCPA. Regardless, I think that the reason that LCPA22-00003 was not forwarded to the Coastal Commission with the LCPA currently under review is that staff figured they would bundle them together and submit one comprehensive Inclusionary Housing LCPA. Unfortunately, it looks like whoever prepared the LCPA currently under review used a version that had 'accepted' all LCPA22-00003 modifications as a starting point, even if those had not been certified by the Commission."

City apologized: "We sincerely apologize for this oversight and are amenable to incorporating LCPA22-00003 into the current LCPA."

## Proposed Changes

1. Increase threshold for inclusionary requirements from 3+ units to 10+ units
2. Increase affordable set-aside from 10% to 15%
3. Require proportionate unit mix, dispersal, and equal amenity access
4. Allow ADUs to satisfy inclusionary requirements

## CCC Pushback on Threshold Increase

CCC questioned the jump from 3 to 10 units as "substantial increase compared to other coastal cities in San Diego County, which have a threshold of 5-7 units."

City response: the 10-unit threshold was recommended by a City Council member at the August 30, 2023 workshop. The 10-unit number came from averaging all California jurisdictions with inclusionary policies.

CCC asked if City would accept a lower threshold. City agreed to reduce to 7 units "to be consistent with Encinitas and Carlsbad."

## Ch. 14C Discrepancies (7 Issues)

CCC's detailed review of the uncertified language raised 7 specific issues:

1. **500 sq ft exemption**: Uncertified language exempted rebuilds expanding by 500 sq ft. CCC noted Coastal Act Section 30610(g) specifies 10%. City agreed to modify for Coastal Act consistency.

2. **Editor's note in 14C.3**: Unnecessary. City agreed to delete.

3. **Income definitions**: "Low-income" used as umbrella term. City clarified it encapsulates very low and extremely low income per Health & Safety Code 50053.

4. **Base density definition deleted**: CCC noted this should be re-incorporated given the new 86 du/acre base density. City deferred to DSD.

5. **Preservation of Existing Affordable Units (14C.8(e))**: CCC flagged as potential loophole. City justified as AB 1505 compliance, citing Encinitas and Carlsbad precedent for alternative means of compliance.

6. **Affordable Housing Plan "may" language (14C.9(a))**: CCC questioned whether "may" created an alternative to density bonus compliance. City clarified: one Affordable Housing Plan can satisfy both inclusionary and density bonus requirements, "may" means the plans can be combined.

7. **CDP requirement for Affordable Housing Agreement**: City agreed to add "prior to issuance of a CDP" to the recording requirement.

## Inclusionary Suggested Modifications

Sent by CCC on January 21, 2026 (2 weeks before hearing). CCC noted the draft "must still undergo the Commission's legal review" but sent the current version because "the staff report must be finalized tomorrow."

Tight timeline: CCC worked through MLK Day weekend to finalize modifications.

## Advocacy Implications

**Threshold increase was political, not analytical.** The 3-to-10 jump came from a City Council member at the August 30, 2023 workshop, not from staff analysis or fiscal feasibility. The stated rationale (average of all CA jurisdictions) is meaningless without controlling for jurisdiction size, housing market, or project pipeline. CCC negotiated it to 7, which is at least peer-consistent (Encinitas, Carlsbad).

**Uncertified ordinance reveals systemic dysfunction.** Not a one-off error. Staff who prepared the current LCPA used an uncertified version as baseline. The City's explanation (staff turnover) is the same explanation given for: why Downtown was excluded from DBL, why traffic monitoring didn't happen, why prior CCC filings were delayed. Pattern: institutional knowledge walks out the door and nobody catches the gap.

**Inclusionary + density cap creates a specific incentive structure.** 15% affordable set-aside (up from 10%) combined with 86 du/acre base density means: developers need DBL to build anything above 86 du/acre. DBL now requires 15% affordable. But SM#3's Coastal Act savings clause lets the City deny DBL waivers on coastal resource grounds. Result: the City can selectively approve or deny density bonuses project-by-project using coastal resource findings. This is discretionary review wearing a Coastal Act costume.

**Etrakit confirms the incentive structure works as designed.** In 2025, the only large downtown project filing is 100% affordable (3296 Mission, 100 units). Market-rate developers cannot make projects pencil at 86 du/acre base; the DBL path to 172 du/acre is uncertain due to Coastal Act review. Only affordable developers (who qualify for deeper DBL concessions and have different capital stacks) are filing.

**CCC staff connected the two LCPAs explicitly.** The density cap creates the floor that makes density bonuses necessary; the inclusionary increase ensures bonuses produce affordable units. This is a coherent policy framework. It is also a framework that structurally advantages affordable-only developers and disadvantages mixed-income market-rate projects. Per the supply-skepticism-rebuttal KB: market-rate housing generates vacancy chains reaching low-income households within 2-6 years (Mast 2023). Blocking market-rate development to force affordable-only production is a net loss for housing supply.
