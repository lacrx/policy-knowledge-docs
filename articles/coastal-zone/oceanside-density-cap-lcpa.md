# CCC-Oceanside Correspondence: Downtown Density Cap LCPA

Source: California Coastal Commission PRA release (October 2026), responding to CPRA #9. ~150 Oceanside-relevant documents spanning February 2022 through January 2026. Cross-verified against etrakit filing data, HCD APR Table A, and `policy-knowledge-docs` KB (ca-housing-enforcement, policy-impact-filing-analysis).

## LCP Amendment Numbers

- **LCP-6-OCN-25-0029-2**: Downtown Density for 6th Housing Cycle (the density cap LCPA)
- **LCP-6-OCN-25-0030-2**: Inclusionary Housing (companion LCPA)
- **LCP-6-OCN-19-0146-3**: The 2019 amendment that removed the 43 du/acre cap and allowed unlimited density
- **LCP-6-OCN-23-0054-2**: Original density cap filing number before resubmission

## Timeline

- **Aug 2019**: City Council removes 43 du/acre cap, allows unlimited density
- **Sep 2021**: CCC certifies unlimited density with suggested modification requiring Traffic Monitoring and Mitigation Plan
- **Feb 2022**: Unlimited density becomes effective
- **Jan 2022**: 712 Seagaze project approved at 319 du/acre (later modified to 497 du/acre when hotel rooms converted to apartments)
- **Oct 2023**: City Council approves 86 du/acre cap
- **Nov 2023**: City submits density cap LCPA to CCC
- **Mar 2024**: CCC files LCPA as complete (delayed from Nov 2023 due to "staffing constraints and workload volume")
- **Aug 2024**: CCC staff drafts report recommending approval AS SUBMITTED (no modifications)
- **Sep 12, 2024**: Item pulled from CCC agenda due to "concerns raised by commissioners"
- **Mar 2025**: City works with CCC staff on revised approach per commissioner guidance
- **Jun 27, 2025**: City resubmits both LCPAs as a package, renames to "Downtown Density for 6th Housing Cycle" (removes word "cap")
- **Aug 20, 2025**: Filed as complete
- **Nov 5, 2025**: One-year time extension granted
- **Nov-Dec 2025**: Intensive Q&A between CCC analyst Belen-Gonzalez and City (Darlene Nicandro)
- **Jan 8, 2026**: CCC sends draft suggested modifications to City
- **Jan 22, 2026**: Staff report finalized (Th16a)
- **Feb 5, 2026**: CCC hearing; certified with 3 suggested modifications

## Key Finding: The September 2024 Pull

CCC staff originally recommended approval AS SUBMITTED in August 2024 (no suggested modifications). Commissioners themselves raised concerns and pulled the item. This is significant: the suggested modifications (converting cap to target, adding DBL provisions) were commissioner-driven, not staff-driven. CCC staff had to go back and develop modifications they hadn't initially thought necessary.

## Key Finding: The Density Bonus Gap

CCC discovered during review that the Downtown District was excluded from density bonus provisions in Article 30 of the IP. When asked why, Darlene Nicandro (Dev Services Director) replied: "We've researched this question and cannot find the answer. Unfortunately the folks who may have worked on that project are no longer with the City."

The City was approving density bonus projects in downtown using State DBL directly, bypassing the certified LCP. City Planner Sergio Madera: "We don't have the staffing resources to keep up with every change in state law. As we're obligated to do, we apply the law as written even if it hasn't been formally incorporated into the City's LCP."

CCC Program Manager Kanani Leslie noted: "it's unclear if the local government could apply changes to DBL that are inconsistent with its currently certified IP, but there is an argument that they could if that application is otherwise consistent with their LCP."

## Three Suggested Modifications

1. **SM#1**: Change "no density limit" to "maximum base density of 86 units per acre, exclusive of any density bonuses." Change 5,500 dwelling unit maximum to a "target" that may be exceeded. (SB 330 compliance)

2. **SM#2**: Change 5,500 cap to target in the Adoption of Mixed-Use Development Plans section. Add language that "any future limit target established by the City's General Plan may be exceeded."

3. **SM#3**: Add density bonus provisions for Downtown District:
   - Coastal Act savings clause: DBL shall not supersede the Coastal Act
   - Minor LCP inconsistencies from DBL may be approved if no significant coastal resource impacts
   - Analysis of coastal resource impacts required for each DBL project
   - City may deny DBL waivers/concessions if significant adverse coastal resource impacts
   - City must update density bonus regulations to include Downtown District by January 1, 2029

## CCC Staff Positions Revealed

- CCC coordinated with HCD to confirm 86 du/acre doesn't violate SB 330 (since 43 du/acre was baseline on Jan 1, 2018)
- Zero RHNA sites are in Downtown District
- CCC staff found the LUP has no policies explicitly mentioning density
- CCC noted 86 du/acre is still 2x+ other SD County coastal cities (Carlsbad 40, Solana Beach 20, Del Mar 25, National City 48)
- CCC used Carlsbad's approach as precedent for converting growth caps to targets
- CCC recommended Morro Bay's DBL language as model for Coastal Act savings clause

## Filing Impact (Cross-Verified)

Etrakit discretionary filing data and APR Table A confirm the density cap killed market-rate downtown development. Full three-check analysis in [oceanside-density-cap-findings](oceanside-density-cap-findings.md).

**Key numbers:**
- Pre-cap peak: 14 downtown housing filings in 2023 (1,465 APR proposed units)
- Post-cap: 35 APR proposed units downtown in 2025. Zero 50+ unit market-rate projects. Only affordable-only and small infill filing.
- SB 330 vesting burst Nov-Dec 2023 confirms developers anticipated infeasibility under 86 du/acre.
- Non-downtown filings steady at 8-9/year, ruling out citywide market downturn.

**SM#3 creates a Coastal Act shield** (per ca-housing-enforcement KB obstruction patterns). The savings clause allows denial of density bonus waivers on coastal resource grounds, routing state mandate overrides through CCC. This extends beyond the density cap itself. It is a permanent mechanism in the certified LCP.

**SB 79 collision (effective July 2026):** Oceanside Transit Center likely qualifies as Tier 1 (120 du/acre, 75ft height). The 86 du/acre cap is below SB 79 Tier 1 density for adjacent parcels. SM#3's Coastal Act savings clause may be used to argue Coastal Act authority supersedes SB 79 transit density mandates. SB 79 does not explicitly address Coastal Act tension. After Jan 1, 2027, denials face HAA presumption of violation in high-resource areas ($10,000/unit minimum, 5x bad faith multiplier). Full analysis in [oceanside-density-cap-findings](oceanside-density-cap-findings.md).

## Christopher Pederson / AB 2097 (Coast Highway)

Attorney Christopher Pederson submitted a comment letter to CCC in April 2023 regarding the Coast Highway Incentive District LCPA (LCP-6-OCN-23-0035-1). Key argument: AB 2097 (Gov. Code 65863.2) prohibits minimum parking requirements within 1/2 mile of major transit stops. Oceanside's main train station and Coast Highway Sprinter station both qualify. Pederson argued parking requirements in the Incentive District are preempted by state law and that the CCC should acknowledge this in suggested modifications.

Pederson also flagged AB 2011 commercial corridor streamlining as potentially applicable to Coast Highway.

CCC Senior Attorney Robin Mayer scheduled a meeting with Pederson to discuss. She noted: "We are aware of the new law."
