# Bike Lane Economic Impact — Evidence Base

## Overview

Business opposition to bike lanes follows a consistent pattern: merchants claim parking removal will kill their businesses. The peer-reviewed evidence consistently shows the opposite — neutral to positive effects on retail sales, foot traffic, and commercial activity. No study has found catastrophic negative impacts.

The strongest finding across studies: **merchants systematically overestimate how many customers drive**, which explains why opposition persists despite positive outcomes.

All claims below survived adversarial verification (3-vote panels, 2/3 threshold required).

---

## Before/After Retail Sales

### Toronto — Bloor Street (Arancibia et al., 2019, JAPA)

Most rigorous study. Replaced 136 on-street parking spots with bike lanes. Before/after surveys with control street (Danforth Avenue).

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| Monthly customer spending | $186 CAD | $245 CAD | +32% |
| Spending >$100/month | 44.2% | 53.3% | p<.001 |
| Merchants with >100 customers/weekday | 33.8% | 55.0% | +21pp |
| Cyclist share of shoppers | 8.0% | 22.0% | ~3x |
| Car share of shoppers | ~9% | ~9% | unchanged |

Control street also saw spending rise. Difference between streets not statistically significant — bike lane definitively did not cause harm despite removing 136 parking spaces.

*DOI: 10.1080/01944363.2019.1638816*

### New York City (NYC DOT, 2014)

Measured via state sales tax receipts vs. nearby control streets.

| Corridor | Infrastructure | Sales Change | Control |
|----------|---------------|-------------|---------|
| 9th Ave, Chelsea | Protected bike lane (2007) | +49% / 3 years | +3% borough-wide |
| Columbus Ave (77th–96th) | Protected lane + ped islands | +20% / 2 years | +9% adjacent, +12% parallel |
| 1st & 2nd Aves | Bike infrastructure | Vacancies -47% | +2% borough-wide |

### San Francisco — Three Corridors (Volker & Handy, 2018, TRR)

Columbus Avenue, Polk Street, Valencia Street. Sales increased on all three. Local-serving businesses showed markedly higher sales on Valencia and Polk.

*DOI: 10.1177/0361198118792321*

### Caltrans Statewide (San Francisco & Alameda Counties)

Class II bike lanes: **no statistically significant change** in sales — except on neighborhood roads (~$44K/year increase). Business characteristics far stronger predictors than bike infrastructure.

*Report CA18-3136*

## The Merchant Perception Gap

The single most important finding for advocacy:

| City | Merchants estimated car share | Actual measured |
|------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| Toronto (Bloor St) | Opposition centered on parking | <10% by car |
| Vancouver | 40% drove | 20% drove |
| Vancouver (cycling) | 4% cycled | 8% cycled |

When a business owner says "my customers need parking," the evidence-based response: "Have you measured how your customers actually arrive?" The answer is almost always no.

## Effects by Business Type

| Business Type | Effect | Confidence |
|---------------|--------|------------|
| Local-serving (cafes, restaurants, bookstores) | Consistently positive | High |
| Auto-dependent (auto repair, drive-throughs) | Understudied | Low |
| Destination retail (specialty, wide draw area) | Depends on parking alternatives | Medium |

Business type and age are far stronger predictors of sales than proximity to bike infrastructure.

## Property Values

Multi-city study, 11 US cities, 2000–2019:

| City | Single-Family | Multifamily |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| Minneapolis | +25% | +20% |
| Columbus | +6% | +22% |
| Portland, SF, Seattle | No significant effect | No significant effect |

Properties at 800m–1200m showed larger premiums than those at 400m.

*Journal of Transport Geography (2025). DOI: 10.1016/j.jtrangeo.2025.103877*

---

## The Only Ambiguous Case

**Calgary**: Spending dropped $153→$131 CAD/month post-bike-lane. But Calgary was in severe oil-price recession with no control group. Methodologically weak — the recession cannot be isolated.

## Common Opposition Patterns

| Claim | Reality | Source |
|-------|---------|--------|
| "Parking removal kills businesses" | No measurable sales effect from parking changes | Caltrans; Bloor Street |
| "Nobody bikes here" | Cycling share often 2-4x merchant estimates | Perception gap studies |
| "My customers all drive" | 90%+ arrive without car in urban contexts | Bloor Street; Vancouver |
| "Sales dropped after the bike lane" | Check for recession, pandemic, or cost crisis | Calgary caveat |
| "Businesses closing because of bike lanes" | Check citywide closure rates | SD 2025-26: 72% report declining traffic |

---

## Evidence Gaps

Claims in either direction should be treated skeptically in these contexts:

1. **Suburban corridors** — nearly all studies from dense urban contexts
2. **Construction-period impacts** — build phase may temporarily harm businesses
3. **Small towns** (<50K population) — almost no peer-reviewed evidence
4. **Protected vs. painted** — infrastructure type not rigorously isolated
5. **Auto-oriented strips** — strip malls, drive-throughs understudied

## Quick Reference

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Do bike lanes kill nearby businesses? | No. | High |
| Do bike lanes help nearby businesses? | Usually yes, sometimes neutral. | High |
| Effects uniform across business types? | No. Local-serving benefits most. | Medium |
| Merchants overestimate car-customer share? | Yes, consistently. | High |
| Bike lanes raise property values? | City-dependent. | Medium |
| Removing parking hurts sales? | No measurable effect. | Medium-High |
| Applies to suburban corridors? | Unknown — evidence gap. | Low |
