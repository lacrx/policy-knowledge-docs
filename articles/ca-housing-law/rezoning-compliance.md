# Housing Element Rezoning Compliance — AB 1398 Deadlines & Enforcement

## Overview

AB 1398 (2021) added teeth to housing element rezoning commitments. Before AB 1398, cities could promise to rezone sites in their housing elements, get HCD certification, then stall indefinitely. AB 1398 creates hard deadlines: miss them, and HCD revokes certification — triggering Builder's Remedy, funding loss, and AG referral simultaneously.

This article covers the rezoning deadline mechanics. For the broader enforcement framework (HAA penalties, SB 35 streamlining, case law), see [ca-housing-enforcement](ca-housing-enforcement.md).

---

## AB 1398 Deadline Structure

Gov. Code § 65583(c)(1)(A) (as amended by AB 1398):

**On-time housing element adoption:**
- Rezoning must be completed within **3 years** of the statutory deadline for adoption

**Late adoption (more than 120 days past statutory deadline):**
- Rezoning must be completed within **1 year** of the statutory deadline

**Bay Area 6th Cycle deadlines (ABAG jurisdictions):**
- Statutory adoption deadline: January 31, 2023
- On-time rezoning deadline: January 31, 2026
- Late-adopter rezoning deadline: January 31, 2024

| Adoption Timing | Rezoning Deadline | Window |
|-----------------|-------------------|--------|
| On time (within 120 days of Jan 31, 2023) | Jan 31, 2026 | 3 years |
| Late (>120 days past Jan 31, 2023) | Jan 31, 2024 | 1 year from statutory deadline |

---

## What "Rezoning" Means Under AB 1398

The statute requires completion of all rezoning commitments made in the housing element's programs — specifically sites identified under Gov. Code § 65583.2(a)(4) to accommodate RHNA shortfalls.

This includes:
- Adopting specific plans or overlay zones that permit residential at committed densities
- Completing CEQA review for programmatic rezonings
- Ensuring zoning is ministerially actionable (not contingent on future discretionary action)

A city cannot satisfy this by merely initiating a planning process. The rezoning must be **adopted and effective**.

---

## Consequences of Missing the Deadline

When a city misses its AB 1398 rezoning deadline, HCD may revoke housing element certification. Revocation triggers cascading penalties:

| Consequence | Mechanism | Impact |
|-------------|-----------|--------|
| **Builder's Remedy** | Gov. Code § 65589.5(d)(5) | Developers can propose projects with ≥20% affordable anywhere, regardless of zoning; city cannot deny |
| **Funding loss** | Multiple state/federal programs | Ineligible for housing, infrastructure, community development, and transportation grants |
| **SB 35 streamlining** | Gov. Code § 65913.4 | Ministerial approval for compliant projects if behind on RHNA |
| **AG referral** | HCD enforcement pipeline | $10K-$50K/month fines |
| **AB 1398 ministerial approval** | Gov. Code § 65583.2(c) | Compliant housing on identified sites gets ministerial (non-discretionary) approval |

These penalties stack. A decertified city faces Builder's Remedy + SB 35 + funding loss + potential AG enforcement simultaneously.

---

## The Delay-and-Comply Pattern

AB 1398 targets a specific obstruction pattern:

```
City identifies site X in housing element for rezoning
    ↓
HCD certifies housing element based on rezoning commitment
    ↓
City drags feet on actual rezoning (extended CEQA, hearings, community process)
    ↓
Years pass with no zoning change
    ↓
City claims compliance because element is "certified"
```

Before AB 1398, this loop could continue indefinitely. HCD had limited tools to enforce rezoning timelines once an element was certified. AB 1398 closes the gap: certification is conditional on delivering the promised rezonings by deadline.

## Case Study: Brisbane Baylands (2005–2026)

Brisbane (pop. ~4,700, San Mateo County) illustrates the full lifecycle of the delay-and-comply pattern — and its consequences under AB 1398.

### Site

The Baylands: 680 acres of former railyard and landfill adjacent to Bayshore Caltrain station, one of the largest undeveloped parcels in the Bay Area. Owned by Universal Paragon Corporation (UPC) since the early 2000s.

### Timeline

**2005–2016: Two decades of resistance**

- 2005: UPC submits initial development proposal
- 2013: City releases Draft EIR analyzing four concept plans. Developer plan (DSP) proposed 4,434 housing units. Community Proposed Plan (CPP) proposed zero housing
- August 2016: Planning Commission unanimously recommends CPP — zero housing. Sustainability statement: "There will be ample housing in the new developments planned across the border in San Francisco"
- October 2016: SF Board of Supervisors introduces resolution to annex Brisbane Baylands into San Francisco in response to zero-housing stance

**2018: Measure JJ**

- July 2018: City Council certifies Final EIR, selects modified plan with 2,200 homes (down from DSP's 4,434)
- November 2018: Measure JJ passes 55.2%–44.8%, amending General Plan to allow 1,800–2,200 residences and up to 7M sq ft commercial
- Housing conditioned on completion of environmental cleanup

**2023: Housing element certified — with rezoning commitment**

- Brisbane's 6th cycle RHNA: 1,588 units (up from 83 in 5th cycle — a 19x increase)
- Housing element identifies Baylands as primary rezoning site to meet RHNA shortfall
- May 2023: HCD certifies Brisbane's housing element, contingent on Baylands rezoning

**2024–2026: Rezoning stalls**

- April 2025: Brisbane files CEQA application for Baylands Specific Development Plan
- September 2025: Draft EIR public comment period closes
- May 2026: AB 1398 rezoning deadline passes. Brisbane has not completed rezoning — still in Planning Commission hearings

**July 2, 2026: HCD revokes certification**

- HCD decertifies Brisbane's housing element for failure to complete rezoning by deadline
- Brisbane publishes press release acknowledging decertification, pledging timeline to regain certification
- Planning Commission hearings continue (next: July 23, 2026)
- City Council consideration expected Fall 2026

### Obstruction Patterns Exhibited

| Pattern | How Brisbane Used It |
|---------|---------------------|
| Environmental pretext | Cited contamination to justify excluding all housing, despite remediation being feasible and underway |
| Community character | 4,700-person city argued 2,200 units would overwhelm town identity |
| Neighbor-will-build-it | Planning Commission: SF will house Baylands workers |
| Promise-then-stall | Got HCD certification with Baylands rezoning commitment, then spent 3 years in CEQA/planning process |
| CEQA as delay | Full EIR cycle (file → draft → comment → final → hearings) consumed the entire rezoning window |

### Key Officials

- **Clarke Conway** (former Mayor): "It was always my hope we'd never put housing out there"
- **Cliff Lentz** (Mayor 2025, 16-year tenure): Called annexation threat "ridiculous"
- **Melinda Coy** (HCD Housing Accountability Unit Chief): Issued the decertification

### Current Exposure

With housing element decertified, Brisbane now faces:
- Builder's Remedy active — developers can bypass local zoning with 20%+ affordable projects
- State/federal funding ineligibility
- Possible AG referral
- Ministerial approval of compliant housing on identified rezoning sites

---

## Generalizable Lessons

1. **Certification is not permanent.** HCD can and will revoke it when rezoning commitments are not met. Brisbane is the precedent.

2. **CEQA timelines don't pause AB 1398 deadlines.** If rezoning requires a specific plan with its own EIR, the city must start early enough to complete both within the AB 1398 window. Brisbane started CEQA in 2025 for a May 2026 deadline.

3. **Large sites are high-risk rezoning commitments.** The larger and more complex the site, the longer CEQA and planning take. Cities that bet their entire RHNA on one mega-site (as Brisbane did with Baylands) create single points of failure for AB 1398 compliance.

4. **19x RHNA increases expose legacy obstruction.** Brisbane's jump from 83 to 1,588 units reflects decades of underproduction. The state's response was to assign allocations that force engagement with sites cities had been blocking.

5. **Promise-then-stall is now time-bounded.** Before AB 1398, housing element promises were aspirational. Now they are contractual, with a hard deadline and automatic penalties.
